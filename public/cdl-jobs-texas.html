CDL jobs in Texas.

More freight moves through Texas than any state but California. If you're based anywhere along I-35, I-10 or I-45, you have leverage — and we'll help you use it.

Texas is the best state in the country to be a CDL-A driver looking for options. The freight base is enormous and it's diverse: oilfield and construction feeding flatbed, produce out of the Rio Grande Valley feeding reefer, retail distribution around Dallas–Fort Worth feeding dry van, and cross-border volume through Laredo that never really slows.

That diversity is why a driver in Texas can be genuinely picky about home time in a way a driver in a thin freight market can't.

Where the freight is

Dallas–Fort Worth is one of the largest inland distribution markets in the US. Enormous warehouse capacity along I-35W and I-20, heavy dry van volume, and enough carriers based there that regional home-weekly work is realistic rather than aspirational.

Houston runs on the port and on petrochemical. That means flatbed, tanker and a lot of drayage. Rates hold up well, and if you're comfortable with the port's pace it is steady year-round work.

Laredo is the busiest inland port on the US–Mexico border. Freight crossing there gets handed to US carriers for the long haul north, which creates constant outbound OTR opportunity — and heavy northbound demand for reefer during produce season.

San Antonio and Austin sit on the I-35 spine between the two, with a growing base of distribution and construction freight.

What Texas carriers typically want

Requirements look much like the rest of the country: CDL-A, a clean or explainable MVR, a current DOT medical card, and 21+ for interstate work. Flatbed accounts around Houston and the Permian will expect securement experience or put you through training.

The advantage of a deep market is that a driver with a record blemish or a SAP history has more doors to knock on here than almost anywhere else.

Home time from a Texas base

Because so many carriers have terminals in-state, regional and home-weekly work is realistically available rather than something you hope for after two years. Drivers based in the DFW area in particular tend to have the widest choice of schedules.

OTR out of Texas pays well and runs long lanes north and east; if you want maximum miles, this is a good place to start from.

What you need to get started

  • CDL-A
  • 21+ for interstate driving
  • Home base in or near Texas
  • Current DOT medical card
  • Securement experience for flatbed accounts, or willingness to train

Common questions

What CDL jobs are available in Texas?

The full range: OTR, regional and dedicated work across dry van, reefer, flatbed and tanker. Texas has one of the deepest freight bases in the country, which is why drivers here usually have more choice than in thinner markets.

Is Texas a good state for truck drivers?

It is one of the best. High freight volume, no state income tax, a low cost of living relative to other large states, and enough carrier terminals in-state that home-weekly work is genuinely available.

Do I need cross-border experience to run Laredo freight?

Usually not. Most US carriers pick up freight after it has crossed and run it north, so you are handling domestic loads. Actually crossing into Mexico is specialised work and a small part of the market.

Which Texas cities have the most trucking jobs?

Dallas–Fort Worth first, then Houston, then San Antonio and Laredo. Being within about an hour of any of these widens your options considerably.

Regional driving keeps you inside a defined footprint — usually a group of neighbouring states — and gets you home most weekends. For drivers with families, it's the segment that makes the job sustainable over years rather than months.

The trade-off is straightforward: fewer miles than OTR, often a slightly higher rate per mile to compensate, and more stops in a typical week.

What counts as regional

There's no single definition. Most carriers mean a radius of roughly 500 to 1,000 miles from your home terminal, or a named group of states — Midwest, Southeast, Northeast. Some describe home-daily work as regional too, though that is usually a dedicated or local account.

Ask exactly which states are in the footprint and how many nights out a normal week involves. “Regional” on a job posting has meant everything from home every night to home every other weekend.

Pay and miles

Regional rates per mile are often a little higher than OTR at the same carrier, because the weekly mileage is lower and there is more loading, unloading and city driving involved. Some regional accounts pay hourly or by the stop rather than by the mile, particularly in grocery and beverage.

Compare weekly take-home, not the headline rate. A regional seat at a higher cents-per-mile but 1,800 miles a week may pay less than an OTR seat at 2,700.

Where regional work is strongest

Regional freight concentrates around distribution hubs and population density — the Midwest triangle, the Southeast corridor, Texas, and the Northeast. If you live near one of those, your options are wide. Further out, the openings are fewer and the footprint gets larger.

Tell us your home city on the application and we'll be honest about what's realistic from where you're based.

What you need to get started

  • CDL-A
  • Typically some verifiable OTR experience, though not always
  • Home base inside or near the carrier's footprint
  • DOT physical and medical card in date
  • Comfortable with more stops and city driving than OTR

Common questions

How often are regional drivers home?

Most regional jobs are home weekly, and some are home every other night depending on the lane. Every offer on our board states the home-time pattern the carrier posted.

Does regional pay less than OTR?

The rate per mile is often slightly higher, but weekly miles are lower, so weekly gross is frequently a little less. Many drivers accept that trade for being home consistently.

Can a new driver get a regional job?

It is harder. Most regional accounts want verifiable OTR experience first, commonly six to twelve months, because the work involves more city driving and customer contact. Some carriers do hire entry-level regional in strong markets.

What if I don't live near a freight hub?

Your options narrow and the footprint tends to widen. Tell us your home city and we will be straight with you about what is realistic rather than sending you to apply for something that will not work.

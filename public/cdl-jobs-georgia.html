CDL jobs in Georgia.

Atlanta distributes for the whole Southeast and Savannah is one of the fastest-growing container ports in the country. Between them, Georgia has become one of the strongest freight markets in the region.

Georgia's freight base grew alongside the Port of Savannah, which has expanded rapidly and pushed a great deal of import volume inland. Atlanta absorbs and redistributes it across the Southeast, backed by one of the largest concentrations of distribution space in the region.

Add a substantial poultry and food industry feeding reefer demand, and there's year-round work across most equipment types.

Where the freight is

Atlanta is the Southeast's distribution centre. The I-75, I-85 and I-20 junction plus a deep warehouse base means regional dry van work is plentiful and home-weekly schedules are common.

Savannah runs on the port. Container volume creates steady drayage and outbound OTR, and inland facilities along I-16 have grown to handle the spillover.

North Georgia carries a significant poultry and food-processing industry, which is why reefer demand in the state is stronger than its size would suggest.

What the work looks like

Regional dry van out of Atlanta typically runs the Southeast — Alabama, Tennessee, the Carolinas, Florida — with home time weekly or better. Port drayage out of Savannah tends to be shorter days with more waiting, and OTR from either city has easy reach up the East Coast and across to Texas.

Atlanta traffic is heavy and deserves the same respect as Chicago's; many carriers schedule around the peak windows rather than fighting them.

Requirements and getting started

Standard CDL-A requirements apply. Port accounts in Savannah may want specific credentials or prior drayage experience; Atlanta regional dry van is one of the easier segments in the Southeast for a driver with a year of clean experience to move into.

Georgia is also a reasonable market for entry-level drivers, since several large carriers with training programmes run terminals in or near the state.

What you need to get started

  • CDL-A
  • Home base in or near Atlanta or Savannah helps
  • Port credentials for some Savannah drayage accounts
  • Current DOT medical card
  • Clean or explainable MVR

Common questions

Is Atlanta a good market for truck drivers?

Yes. It is the Southeast's main distribution hub, with a deep warehouse base and a junction of three interstates, so regional work with weekly home time is widely available.

What kind of trucking jobs are there in Savannah?

Mostly port-driven: container drayage from the terminal to inland facilities, plus outbound OTR carrying import freight north and west. Some accounts require port credentials.

Is there reefer work in Georgia?

More than the state's size suggests, because of the large poultry and food-processing industry in north Georgia. Reefer demand there is steady year-round.

Can a new CDL driver find work in Georgia?

Yes. Several large carriers with training programmes operate terminals in or near the state, which makes it a reasonable place to start a first year.
